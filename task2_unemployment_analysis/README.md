## 🧹 Data Cleaning Performed
- Stripped whitespace from column names and values (the raw CSVs have padded headers like ` Region,`)
- Standardized the two files' different column names into one schema (`Unemployment Rate (%)`, `Employed`, `Labour Participation Rate (%)`)
- Parsed `Date` with `dayfirst=True` (dates are DD-MM-YYYY) and dropped unparseable rows
- Resolved the duplicated `Region` column in the 2020 file into a proper `Zone` (East/West/North/South/Northeast)
- Removed duplicates; verified **0 missing values** remain in the 740 clean rows covering 28 states

## 📊 Key Results (from the actual run)

### Covid-19 impact — the central finding
| Metric | Pre-lockdown (May 2019–Mar 2020) | Lockdown (Apr–Nov 2020) |
|---|---|---|
| Avg unemployment rate | **9.51%** | **17.78%** (+8.27 pp, +87%) |
| Labour Participation Rate | 43.89% | 39.32% (−4.57 pp) |

- National monthly peak: **24.88% (May 2020)** — right after the 25 Mar 2020 nationwide lockdown
- Estimated Employed across 28 states fell **31.8%** between Feb 2020 and Apr 2020
- The drop in Labour Participation Rate means many people **stopped looking for work entirely** — hidden unemployment beyond the headline rate

### Patterns found
- **Urban > rural unemployment on average** (13.17% vs 10.32%) — city lockdowns shut services and informal work
- **Extreme state disparity:** Tripura (28.4%) and Haryana (26.3%) average vs Meghalaya (4.8%); Puducherry peaked at a staggering **76.7%**
- **Seasonality:** unemployment builds through mid-year and spikes with the lockdown; the pre-Covid months show a milder summer peak

## 💡 Policy Insights
1. Pandemic shocks demand **automatic stabilizers** (unemployment insurance, cash transfers) that trigger fast — the April 2020 shock was instant and massive
2. **Urban informal workers** need priority protection: they were hit hardest and have the thinnest buffers
3. High-unemployment states (Tripura, Haryana, Jharkhand, Bihar) need **state-targeted** job programs, not uniform national policy
4. The LFPR collapse shows headline unemployment **understates** the damage — participation recovery matters too
