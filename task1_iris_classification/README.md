## 🧠 Classification Concepts Demonstrated
- **Features & labels:** the 4 flower measurements are features; the species name is the label
- **Train/test split:** 80% of flowers train the model, 20% are held out to measure real performance
- **Stratification:** keeps the 50/50/50 species ratio in both splits
- **Feature scaling:** `StandardScaler` puts all features on the same scale (important for KNN and SVM)
- **Three classifiers compared:** Logistic Regression, K-Nearest Neighbors (k=5), SVM (RBF kernel)
- **Evaluation metrics:** accuracy, per-class precision/recall/F1, confusion matrices, and 5-fold cross-validation to check the results are stable, not a lucky split

## 📊 Results Summary
Accuracy on the held-out test set (30 flowers, stratified, seed 42):

| Model | Test Accuracy | 5-Fold CV |
|-------|---------------|-----------|
| SVM (RBF kernel) | **0.9667** | 0.967 ± 0.021 |
| Logistic Regression | 0.9333 | 0.960 ± 0.039 |
| KNN (k=5) | 0.9333 | 0.960 ± 0.025 |

**Key findings (from the actual run):**
- All three models reach ~93–97% test accuracy and ~96% cross-validated accuracy — petal measurements alone nearly separate the species
- *Setosa* is perfectly separable: every model classifies it with 100% precision/recall
- The only errors occur between *versicolor* and *virginica* (e.g., SVM misclassified 1 versicolor as virginica) — their measurement ranges genuinely overlap
- Petal length & petal width carry almost all the predictive signal (correlation heatmap); sepal width is nearly useless alone
- The PCA projection shows why accuracy is high: species form clearly separated clusters in 2-D (PC1 alone explains 73% of variance)
